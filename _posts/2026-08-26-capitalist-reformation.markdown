Economists know that stationarity assumptions breakdown.  Much of modern econometrics (GARCH, regime-switching models, structural break tests, local stationarity, co-integration-with-breaks, and so on) responds to the "statistics change when the regime changes."  Even the best non-stationary-aware econometrics still fits something (a break-point distribution, a time-varying volatility parameter, a regime-transition-probability), and still assumes that whatever regime you are currently holds at least locally.  There is still a model, still a fit, still an implicit "the recent past predicts the local future."



Scientific Machine Learning (SML) argues that regression-to-mean was never the right tool for what Smith described in the first place, that the correction is caused by something, not merely statistically likely.  The SML differentiator isn't stationary vs. non-stationary, but rather estimated vs. deduced.
